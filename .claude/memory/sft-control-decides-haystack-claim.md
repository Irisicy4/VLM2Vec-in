RESOLVED. `sft-local-s0` (relevance-SFT, gme2b, pool_train_1M, 1200 steps, no index in training) was
cross-evaluated on built/corpus_mix2M.jsonl on 2026-09-16:

    arm on the 2M index          acc    eR@1   eR@5   eR@10  eR@20  aR@5   aR@10
    base (frozen)              25.13    6.23  14.27  19.30  25.60   9.96  14.04
    relevance-SFT, 422K-matched 36.47  49.83  69.07  75.40  80.80  54.49  65.35
    ours, trained 422K          41.93  46.77  69.83  76.00  82.60  57.88  65.96

**Haystack robustness is NOT a property of the indirect reward.** SFT gains +54.80 entity R@5 over the
base and ties the indirect arm (gap 0.77 vs a 2.03 floor) despite never building an index in training.
Never argue the +55-point robustness as something the indirect objective earns.

SFT is *better* at entity R@1 (49.83 vs 46.77, gap 3.07 vs a 1.97 floor) — relevance supervision
optimises the argmax; a Plackett-Luce list action optimises a slate, so ours gains at depth instead.

**What survives is the downstream advantage, and only that:** accuracy +5.47 (floor 1.83), answer R@5
+3.39 (floor 2.32), entity R@20 +1.80 (floor 1.43); entity R@5 and R@10 are ties. Same shape as the
in-domain table, now under a 4.7x larger mixed-domain index. Single seed per haystack row.

**How to apply:** the usable claim is "the reader-success advantage persists under a much harder index",
not "indirect feedback is robust to haystack growth". Table is `tab_mm_ood.tex` in
/raid/icy/Overleaf_Indirect, regenerated by `mmrag/gen_tab_mm_ood.py`. Mechanism in
