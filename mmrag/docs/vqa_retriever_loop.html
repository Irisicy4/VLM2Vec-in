Method · annotation-free retrieval RL · multimodal twin

The VQA Retriever Loop

The multimodal encoder is the policy, a frozen vision-language reader is the environment, and the reward is whether that reader answers a visual question correctly from the passages the retriever chose — while looking at the same image the retriever embedded. No relevance label is used anywhere; the image is never annotated at all.

The working recipe

Of everything tried, the arm that reliably clears the frozen base is the loop above with every regularizer removed — no contrastive anchor, no KL, a critic-free group baseline and nothing else. The MM twin wins by subtraction where the text twin wins by substitution:

estimator
PL-GRPO — G=4 sampled lists of k=4 over a top-24 support, group-relative advantage, no critic
anchor
contrastive_coef: 0.0 — the InfoNCE term is off (v2 kept it at 0.3: more recall, less accuracy)
regularizer
kl_beta: 0.0 — none. The stability the text side buys with KL, this side gets from the base
credit
per-document — each unique sampled doc is read once; a list's reward is the mean over its docs. The joint-context scalar variant collapsed
learning rate
2e-5 constant, 500 steps ≈ 2k query draws; consumption beyond that plateaus (34.00 ± 0.47 → 34.80 → 34.07 at 2k/6k/12k draws)
reproduce

labels used
none — answer strings only, --no_force_gold; the image is never annotated

The pieces, concretely

policy
GME-Qwen2-VL-2B, joint image+text embedder, LoRA r=32 α=64 on attention + MLP projections
state
one (image, question) pair from InfoSeek — no history, single hop
action
an ordered 4-passage list drawn from the policy's own top-24
sampling
Plackett-Luce over q·d/τ at τ=0.02 — sampling without replacement, exact list log-prob
environment
Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, frozen, sees the image + each retrieved passage, generates 4 answers at T=0.7
reward
fraction of the 4 rollouts that cover-match the alias set → r ∈ {0, ¼, ½, ¾, 1}, per unique document
baseline
mean/std over the G=4 lists of the same query. No value head (vf_coef: 0)
batch
4 queries × 4 lists; ~16 unique docs × 4 rollouts ≈ 64 reader generations per gradient step
index drift
the 3,000-distractor passage index is re-encoded with current weights every 100 steps
benchmark
held-out InfoSeek: retrieval over the full corpus, top-5 passages to a frozen Qwen2.5-VL-7B reader (train and eval readers deliberately differ)

Where it diverges from standard GRPO

componentstandard GRPO (LLM RLHF)this loop
action token sequence, autoregressive ordered passage list; log-prob is a Plackett-Luce product over 4 picks, not a token chain
action space the whole vocabulary at every step 24 candidates the policy itself retrieved — the policy defines its own action set, and no gradient flows through that selection
reward learned reward model or verifier on the policy's own output a second frozen model's task accuracy, conditioned on the same image the policy embedded — the retriever is never scored directly, only through the reader's eyes
group baselinez-score over G samples, no criticidentical (G=4)
KL to reference β·KL to the frozen SFT policy — usually essential absent. The winning arm carries no KL and no anchor; a strong retrieval-finetuned base is the de-facto trust region
PPO clip binds when the policy drifts across inner epochs present in code but inert — one gradient step per sampled batch means the ratio is 1 by construction
episode multi-token, credit assigned along the sequence single action, single reward — a contextual bandit; the only credit assignment is list-to-document, done by per-document reads
environment drift none; the environment is the reward model the passage index lags the policy by up to 100 steps of weight updates

Is it proper RL?

genuinely RL

The objective is not differentiable and never becomes so: the reward exists on the far side of a frozen VLM's sampled generations and a string match. Actions are sampled, their exact log-probability is known, the baseline is a proper within-group variance reduction, and the gradient reaches only the adapter. The full policy-gradient contract, on an environment you genuinely cannot backprop through.

not PPO, and the clip is decoration

One gradient step per sampled batch means old_logps come from the very weights being updated — the importance ratio is 1 and the clip never binds. The honest name is REINFORCE with a group baseline over Plackett-Luce list actions. Same degeneracy as the text twin; neither side's trust region does any work.

not sequential RL

One state, one action, one reward — a contextual bandit. No bootstrapping, no discounting, no transitions. "RL" here means policy-gradient optimization of a black-box objective.

the caveat that actually bites

Exploration is bounded by the thing being trained: the policy ranks inside its own top-24, so a passage the base never surfaces can never be rewarded. The method is a reranking correction, not a retrieval-capability gain — and it predicts the scope we measured: gains on retrieval-finetuned bases that are nearly right, catastrophe elsewhere.

the base model is part of the recipe

This is the sharpest MM-specific scope condition, measured across ten bases: only encoders already finetuned for retrieval can learn from this reward (GME-2B/7B; BGE-VL at 1e-5, +5.5 R@5, 1 seed). Every raw VLM tested destroys its retrieval while training — full-diversity drift (VLM2Vec 2B and 7B, robust across a 4× LR range) or top-5 contraction to as few as 32 distinct lists in 3,000 queries (CLIP family, raw Qwen2.5-VL at every rate). The learning rate slides the failure mode; it does not change the verdict. And accuracy alone cannot see any of this: full contraction drives VQA accuracy up, to exactly the no-context floor — three cells "improved" while their retrieval died. Retrieval metrics or nothing.

"annotation-free" has a precise boundary

No relevance label, no gold passage, and — the MM-specific part — no image annotation: no region, no caption, no visual grounding of any kind. The image is supervision-free on both of its two entries into the loop. What the reward does need is the question's answer string with aliases. Free of retrieval and visual supervision, not free of supervision.

Short answer: yes — the same policy-gradient contract as the text twin, with the image entering twice (embedded by the policy, judged against by the environment) and labeled never. It is not PPO, it is not sequential, and its action space is small and self-defined. The accurate one-liner is “Plackett-Luce GRPO over passage lists with an image-conditioned frozen-reader reward, no regularizer” — and the scientific content is in what the reward can and cannot teach: it upgrades retrievers that already retrieve, transfers out of domain where supervised finetuning anti-transfers, and destroys everything else while the accuracy metric applauds.
